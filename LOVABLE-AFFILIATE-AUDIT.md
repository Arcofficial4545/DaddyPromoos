## One paragraph, in my voice

I want to be fair to this applicant, because the underlying editorial work is better than most of what crosses my desk: the reviews name the catch, the comparisons refuse the premise where the premise is wrong, the structured data emits a single-author `Review` instead of a fake `AggregateRating`, the coupon "works rate" is gated behind a real vote threshold, there is no pixel, no cookie stuffing, no auto-redirect, and the footer carries a proper nominative-use trademark disclaimer. That is a real property, not a coupon farm. But the live site's headline is "Tested. Scored. Worth buying." and its About page tells my prospective customers that "a deal does not ship until an editor has confirmed it applies at checkout," while the applicant's own repository comment concedes that "every verdict on this site is desk research against official docs and pricing, not a hands-on lab test," and their seed script manufactures the verification timestamps with `days(-2)`. That is desk research wearing a lab coat, and it is exactly the claim I get called about when a partner's review page is screenshotted next to a refund request. Add an automated fetcher that pulls lovable.dev's homepage with a spoofed Chrome user agent, downloads our OG marketing banner, and renders it in a strip labelled product imagery — on a site whose methodology page says "No automated scrapers" — and I have a partner whose stated standards and shipped behaviour do not match. Commercially it is moot anyway: not one outbound link on this site carries a tracking parameter, so on today's build the program would pay them exactly $0 no matter how well they converted. Rejected, with a fix list that is genuinely achievable in a fortnight.

# Phase 2 — Soft signals

### 10. Factual accuracy about Lovable — 6/10

**Right:** The free-tier credit model in [scripts/seed.ts:246](scripts/seed.ts#L246) ("5 build credits/day (up to 30/mo) plus 20 monthly Cloud credits") matches `lovable.dev/pricing` verbatim as of today. The GitHub sync, Supabase backend, and code-ownership framing are accurate. The paid-tier row honestly declines to state a price and tells the reader to confirm on our page. The annual-billing offer is explicitly marked unverified in its own terms. This is more careful than most partners.

**Wrong:** The live "Verified"-badged headline offer reads **"Free tier — daily messages, real code output"** ([scripts/seed.ts:1191](scripts/seed.ts#L1191)). Our free tier is credit-metered, not message-metered; "daily messages" describes a model we no longer run. A stale factual claim carrying a verification badge is the exact combination that produces a support ticket and then a reversal.

**Missing:** The live Lovable page renders no pricing table and no review date at all — `grep` for "Pricing as of" and "Reviewed as of" in the fetched HTML returns nothing. A 9.2/10 score with no date attached is worse than a stale date. The repo content that would fix this has not reached production.

### 11. Link hygiene — 8/10

# Phase 3 — Commercial fit

**Is it worth account manager time?** No — not today. Working this application to approval costs me a compliance review, a remediation cycle, and a re-review, against a best case of roughly $240/month twelve months out and a realistic case of under $50/month next quarter. The upside is that the editorial quality is real and the traffic problem is the fixable kind. I would rather see them again in a quarter with a custom domain, honest claims, and some measurable traffic than approve a partner whose live homepage says "stress-test" about work nobody performed.
